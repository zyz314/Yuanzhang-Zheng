EquiBind

Fast SE(3)-equivariant geometric deep learning for blind ligand docking.

Year
2022
Difficulty
Intermediate
Compute
GPU recommended
Status
Brief ready

Access note. Useful historical baseline

What it does

EquiBind predicts a ligand pose directly from protein and ligand structure, without a search over poses. It is one of the early geometric deep-learning docking models.

It is fast and blind, but less accurate than diffusion-based docking and more likely to produce steric clashes.

The AI principle

EquiBind uses an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network. It predicts atom-level correspondences and a rigid-body transformation that places the ligand in the pocket.

There is no diffusion process and no physics-based scoring. The model learns a direct mapping from structure to pose, which makes it fast but limits its ability to represent uncertainty.

What it is good at

  • It is very fast and does not require a pocket definition.
  • The equivariant architecture is a clean example of geometric deep learning.
  • It is a useful baseline for newer diffusion dockers.

Where it breaks

  • Accuracy and physical validity lag behind DiffDock and physics-based docking.
  • It assumes a rigid ligand and rigid protein.
  • It provides little uncertainty information.

How I would improve it

  • Add a diffusion or refinement stage to fix clashes.
  • Model ligand flexibility and protein side-chain flexibility.
  • Calibrate confidence and report PoseBusters validity.
