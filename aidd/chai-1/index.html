What it does

Chai-1 predicts biomolecular complexes from sequences and chemical inputs. It is aimed at the same broad co-folding problem as AlphaFold3 and Boltz.

It produces structures and confidence scores, not binding affinities or experimental validation.

The AI principle

Chai-1 uses a transformer-based trunk with pair representations and a diffusion-based structure module. It learns to co-fold multiple chains and ligands jointly rather than docking rigid partners.

The model's confidence outputs summarize local and interface quality. As with all co-folding models, confidence is a model score, not a probability of experimental success.

What it is good at

  • It is open and covers a wide range of biomolecular inputs.
  • It is competitive with other open co-folding models on standard complexes.
  • It is useful as an independent check on Boltz predictions.

Where it breaks

  • Licensing and weight terms have been reported inconsistently; check the repository before commercial use.
  • It does not model binding affinity, protein dynamics, or covalent chemistry in full.
  • Accuracy drops on unseen ligand chemistry and flexible interfaces.

How I would improve it

  • Add a calibrated affinity/kinetics head.
  • Support ensemble prediction and explicit uncertainty for flexible loops.
  • Publish a clear, stable license and weight card.
