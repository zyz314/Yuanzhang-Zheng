FrameDiff

SE(3) diffusion on rigid frames for protein backbone generation.

Year
2023
Difficulty
Advanced
Compute
GPU
Status
Brief ready

Access note. Geometric diffusion baseline

What it does

FrameDiff generates protein backbones by diffusing rigid frames. It is an early and influential SE(3) diffusion model.

It outputs backbones only; sequence design and validation are separate.

The AI principle

FrameDiff represents each residue as a rigid frame and performs diffusion on the SE(3) manifold. The denoiser predicts frame updates, and the reverse process produces a consistent backbone.

The model learns geometric constraints without requiring a protein language model. It is a pure structure-generative approach.

What it is good at

  • It is conceptually clean and a good introduction to SE(3) diffusion.
  • It generates diverse backbones without sequence conditioning.
  • It is lightweight compared with all-atom models.

Where it breaks

  • It has no sequence or function conditioning.
  • Designability and experimental success are limited.
  • It has been superseded by RFdiffusion, Chroma, and Genie for most practical tasks.

How I would improve it

  • Add motif and target conditioning.
  • Couple with sequence design and folding filters.
  • Move to all-atom representations for chemistry.
