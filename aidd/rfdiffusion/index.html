RFdiffusion

The original SE(3) diffusion model for protein backbone design, motif scaffolding, and binder design.

Year
2023
Difficulty
Intermediate
Compute
GPU recommended
Status
Brief ready

Access note. Classic baseline for binder design

What it does

RFdiffusion generates protein backbones. It can scaffold a motif, design a binder against a target, or generate an unconditional protein. It is the foundation for many later design pipelines, including RFantibody.

It outputs a backbone, not a sequence. ProteinMPNN or another inverse-folding model is required to assign amino acids.

The AI principle

RFdiffusion performs denoising diffusion over rigid protein frames in SE(3). The forward process adds noise to residue frames; the reverse process uses a RoseTTAFold-derived denoiser to predict the clean structure.

Conditioning is implemented by fixing the target and motif frames during denoising. The model learns to generate the rest of the backbone so that it packs against the fixed context.

What it is good at

  • It is a proven, widely used baseline for binder and motif design.
  • The SE(3) formulation respects rotational and translational symmetry.
  • It has a large ecosystem of examples, filters, and downstream tools.

Where it breaks

  • Backbone-only generation ignores side-chain and ligand chemistry.
  • Experimental success rates are modest; in-silico filters are imperfect.
  • It requires substantial GPU time for large campaigns.

How I would improve it

  • Move to all-atom conditioning for ligands and catalytic side chains, as RFdiffusion2/3 do.
  • Calibrate filters against experimental positives and negatives.
  • Report diversity and failure modes, not only top designs.
