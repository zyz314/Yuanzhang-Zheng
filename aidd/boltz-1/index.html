What it does

Boltz-1 predicts structures of protein, ligand, and nucleic-acid complexes. It was released as an open, reproducible alternative to AlphaFold3 at a time when AF3 weights were not generally available.

It is a structure model. It does not predict affinity or dynamics.

The AI principle

Boltz-1 uses a pairformer-style trunk and a diffusion module to generate all-atom coordinates. It learns from complex structures and predicts the joint conformation of the interacting molecules.

The model's confidence heads estimate local and pairwise accuracy. Those scores are useful for filtering, but they are not calibrated binding probabilities.

What it is good at

  • Open code and weights make it a practical baseline for complex prediction.
  • It handles proteins, ligands, and nucleic acids in one framework.
  • It is a good comparison point for Boltz-2 and Chai-1.

Where it breaks

  • It does not output affinity; a separate model is needed for binding energy.
  • Ligand and ion chemistry can be mishandled, especially for unusual cofactors.
  • Static structures miss conformational ensembles and induced fit.

How I would improve it

  • Add an affinity head and calibrate it against matched experimental series.
  • Improve ligand chemistry and protonation handling.
  • Report ensemble uncertainty rather than a single pose.
