DiffPharma

Interaction-constrained 3D molecular generation for structure-based pharmacophores.

What it does

DiffPharma generates molecules under explicit protein-ligand interaction constraints. It is intended for structure-based pharmacophore modeling, where the goal is not just to fit a pocket but to reproduce specific interactions.

It outputs 3D candidate molecules that can be docked, scored, and compared with the pharmacophore hypothesis used to generate them.

The AI principle

The model is a diffusion generator over 3D molecular structures. The condition is an interaction/pharmacophore specification: hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, or other geometric constraints derived from the target.

Training teaches the denoiser to produce molecules whose atoms and functional groups satisfy those constraints. The interesting part is the explicit interface between medicinal-chemistry constraints and the generative process.

What it is good at

  • The constraints are interpretable, which makes failures easier to diagnose than with a black-box pocket score.
  • It targets structure-based pharmacophores, a practical concept in lead discovery.
  • It generates 3D molecules directly, so shape and interaction geometry are part of the output.

Where it breaks

  • The quality of the output is bounded by the quality of the pharmacophore hypothesis.
  • A molecule can satisfy the constraints and still be unstable, unsynthesizable, or non-selective.
  • Protein flexibility and induced fit are not fully represented by a static constraint set.

How I would improve it

  • Condition on an ensemble of pocket conformations rather than a single crystal structure.
  • Add synthesizability and ADMET constraints during generation or as a learned filter.
  • Benchmark against a medicinal-chemistry team's real pharmacophore queries, not only retrospective sets.
