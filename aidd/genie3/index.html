Genie3

SE(3)-equivariant all-atom diffusion for fast protein backbone and binder design.

Year
2026
Difficulty
Advanced
Compute

Status
Brief ready

What it does

Genie3 generates protein structures. It can make unconditional monomers, scaffold one or more functional motifs, and design binders against a target interface.

It is a structure generator, not a full design pipeline. The paper pairs it with ProteinMPNN for sequences and AlphaFold-based filters for in-silico evaluation.

The AI principle

Genie3 represents proteins as branched polymers. In the forward process it adds noise to atom point clouds; in the reverse process it reasons over Frenet-Serret frames along the backbone and along each side chain. This keeps the model SE(3)-equivariant while still giving it all-atom detail.

The denoiser uses an SE(3)-invariant input embedder, a latent transformer with bidirectional single/pair updates, and an equivariant structural decoder with invariant point attention. For motifs, known side-chain atoms are atomized; the scaffold is generated at backbone level. Training includes multimers, which is what enables binder design.

What it is good at

  • It keeps exact rotational and translational equivariance, unlike many recent all-atom models that rely on augmentation.
  • It is fast: the paper reports a 10x reduction in sampling steps with little quality loss.
  • It handles motif scaffolding and binder design in one architecture, with strong in-silico results.

Where it breaks

  • The experimental validation is still limited; most evaluation is in-silico self-consistency.
  • It is a protein-only model. Ligands and nucleic acids are outside its native representation.
  • The binder-design filter can still select designs that bind the wrong epitope or fail in the lab.

How I would improve it

  • Extend the branched-polymer representation to ligands, nucleic acids, and metals.
  • Calibrate the in-silico filter against experimental positives and negatives, not only AF2/AF3 confidence.
  • Report design diversity and failure modes alongside success rates.
