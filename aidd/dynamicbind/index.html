DynamicBind

Diffusion-based docking with protein flexibility and induced fit.

Year
2023
Difficulty
Advanced
Compute
GPU recommended
Status
Brief ready

Access note. Paper and setup in repository

What it does

DynamicBind predicts a protein-ligand complex while allowing the protein structure to adjust. It is aimed at induced fit, where a rigid-receptor docking model would miss the relevant pocket.

It outputs a complex structure and confidence scores. It is not a molecular-dynamics simulation and does not provide kinetics.

The AI principle

DynamicBind uses an equivariant diffusion model over the protein-ligand complex. The protein and ligand are denoised together, so the model can generate a holo-like pocket rather than keeping the apo structure fixed.

The model conditions on the protein sequence/structure and the ligand, learning a joint distribution of conformations. This is a harder problem than rigid docking and requires more compute.

What it is good at

  • It explicitly addresses protein flexibility, a major failure mode of rigid docking.
  • It can generate a pocket that is not open in the input structure.
  • It is a useful comparison against DiffDock and Vina on flexible targets.

Where it breaks

  • It is expensive and can generate unrealistic protein distortions.
  • Validation is mostly retrospective; prospective hit rates are less clear.
  • It does not model explicit water, ions, or long-timescale conformational changes.

How I would improve it

  • Constrain protein flexibility with physical potentials or normal-mode priors.
  • Validate prospectively on cryptic-pocket targets.
  • Add affinity and residence-time prediction.
