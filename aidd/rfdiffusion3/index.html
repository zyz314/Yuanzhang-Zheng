What it does

RFdiffusion3 is the all-atom branch of the RFdiffusion family. It is built for design problems where the protein does not exist in isolation: an enzyme around a cofactor, a binder around a small molecule, or a protein that must pack against nucleic acid or a metal site.

It proposes protein structures under atom-level constraints, then leaves sequence design and validation to the rest of the pipeline. It is a design model, not a structure predictor or an experimental oracle.

The AI principle

The model is a denoising diffusion model over protein geometry. Training adds noise to protein structures; a RoseTTAFold-derived network learns to predict that noise and remove it. At sampling time, the model starts from noise and gradually resolves a structure that satisfies the supplied condition.

The important change in RFdiffusion3 is the all-atom representation. Instead of reasoning only over backbone frames, the model represents each residue with a tryptophan-sized atom cloud (4 backbone atoms and 10 side-chain atoms), collapsing unused atoms onto the beta carbon. This lets the denoiser see side-chain geometry and non-protein atoms, not just the C-alpha trace.

What it is good at

  • It can condition on ligands, nucleic acids, metals, and catalytic side chains, which backbone-only models cannot represent directly.
  • The all-atom representation is a better match for enzyme active sites and cofactor-binding pockets.
  • It fits inside the Foundry/RosettaCommons ecosystem, so it can be paired with ProteinMPNN, RF2, and standard design filters.

Where it breaks

  • All-atom generation is expensive and gets harder as the number of ligand atoms and protein residues grows.
  • A generated structure can be geometrically plausible but chemically wrong: strained bonds, incorrect protonation, or impossible metal coordination.
  • The model does not by itself prove expression, folding, binding, or activity. Those require sequence design and wet-lab tests.

How I would improve it

  • Add explicit chemistry and coordination terms to the denoiser or the sampler, rather than hoping the model learns them from structures.
  • Train and evaluate on matched ligand-bound complexes, with a held-out set organized by ligand scaffold rather than random split.
  • Pair generation with a physics-aware filter (PoseBusters, Rosetta, or short MD) and report failures, not just top designs.
