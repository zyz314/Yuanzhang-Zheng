ProteinMPNN

Message-passing inverse folding: a sequence for a fixed protein backbone.

Year
2022
Difficulty
Beginner
Compute
GPU recommended
Status
Brief ready

Access note. Practical companion to RFdiffusion

What it does

ProteinMPNN takes a protein backbone and designs amino-acid sequences that are likely to fold into it. It is the standard sequence-design step after RFdiffusion, Chroma, or Genie.

It does not change the backbone. If the backbone is wrong, ProteinMPNN cannot rescue it.

The AI principle

ProteinMPNN is an encoder-decoder graph neural network. The encoder passes messages between residues and atoms in the backbone; the decoder predicts amino acids in a random order, so the model is not tied to an N-to-C generation order.

Training uses masked sequence recovery on PDB structures. The model learns local packing rules and longer-range dependencies, but it does not explicitly model folding thermodynamics.

What it is good at

  • It is fast, robust, and easy to run at scale.
  • Order-agnostic decoding improves sequence diversity and avoids left-to-right bias.
  • It has become a de facto standard in protein-design pipelines.

Where it breaks

  • It is only as good as the input backbone.
  • It does not model ligands, nucleic acids, or explicit solvent by default.
  • High sequence recovery does not guarantee expression, stability, or function.

How I would improve it

  • Add ligand and cofactor context, as LigandMPNN does.
  • Condition on expression host and developability constraints.
  • Evaluate designs with experimental stability and binding data, not only recovery.
