Boltz-2

Open biomolecular structure prediction with a binding-affinity head.

Year

What it does

Boltz-2 predicts the structure of protein complexes and estimates binding affinity. It is one of the few open models that tries to connect a predicted pose to a quantitative binding signal.

It can be used for co-folding, pose inspection, and early affinity triage. It is not a replacement for experimental affinity measurements or free-energy calculations.

The AI principle

Boltz-2 follows the AlphaFold3 design pattern: a pairformer trunk builds residue-pair representations, a diffusion module generates atom coordinates, and a confidence head estimates uncertainty.

The affinity module is the distinctive part. It learns from protein-ligand binding data and produces a predicted affinity/IC50-like score. The structure and affinity heads share representations but are not the same task, so calibration matters.

What it is good at

  • It is open and practical to run, with MIT code and weights according to the repository.
  • It predicts complexes and affinity in one pass, which is useful for triage.
  • It handles proteins, ligands, and some ions/nucleic acids, making it a broad baseline.

Where it breaks

  • Affinity prediction is noisy and can be poorly calibrated outside the training distribution.
  • A static structure does not capture entropy, residence time, or induced fit.
  • The model can be confidently wrong on novel chemistry or difficult targets.

How I would improve it

  • Calibrate affinity per target family and report uncertainty intervals, not a single number.
  • Add ensemble/entropy estimates and kinetics-aware labels.
  • Evaluate on prospective, matched-series data rather than random splits.
