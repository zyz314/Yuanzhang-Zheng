GenMol

A single discrete-diffusion model for de novo generation, fragment-constrained design, and lead optimization.

Year

Difficulty

Compute

Status

Access note. Repository also describes GenMol V2

What it does

GenMol generates molecules as SAFE (Sequential Attachment-based Fragment Embedding) sequences. It is a generalist: the same model handles de novo design, fragment-constrained generation, goal-directed hit generation, and lead optimization.

It is a 2D molecular generator. It does not see the protein pocket unless a separate docking or scoring step is added.

The AI principle

GenMol uses masked discrete diffusion over SAFE tokens. Instead of generating left-to-right like a GPT, it denoises many tokens in parallel, so the molecule is not tied to a single token order.

Fragments are the basic building blocks. Fragment remasking lets the model regenerate selected fragments during optimization, and molecular context guidance (MCG) steers the denoising toward a desired property or context.

What it is good at

  • One model covers several drug-discovery tasks, reducing pipeline fragmentation.
  • Parallel decoding is efficient and avoids some of the exposure bias of autoregressive generation.
  • Fragment-level editing is a natural fit for lead optimization.

Where it breaks

  • It is not structure-conditioned by default; pocket-aware design needs an external docking loop.
  • Validity and novelty are not the same as synthesizability or biological activity.
  • Property guidance can collapse diversity if the reward is too narrow.

How I would improve it

  • Add 3D pocket conditioning or a learned docking surrogate to the denoising process.
  • Include synthesizability and ADMET constraints during generation.
  • Report diversity and failure modes alongside top scores.
