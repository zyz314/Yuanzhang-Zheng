What it does

LaMGen is a 2026 research model for generating 3D molecules against more than one target. It is aimed at polypharmacology and multi-target design, where a single molecule must satisfy several pocket constraints.

The public record is thinner than for the older open-source models. The paper and a Zenodo checkpoint are available, but I have not yet verified a maintained GitHub repository.

The AI principle

The model combines a language-model prior over molecular representations with 3D geometry and multi-target conditioning. The exact architecture should be read from the paper before reproduction; the checkpoint release suggests a sequence/token-based generator rather than a pure equivariant diffusion model.

The key AI idea is conditional generation under multiple, sometimes conflicting, objectives. The model must learn a joint distribution over molecules that can dock into several pockets, not just optimize one score.

What it is good at

  • Multi-target conditioning is a real gap in most open-source molecular generators.
  • It produces 3D candidates, so the output can be docked and checked geometrically.
  • It is a useful test case for polypharmacology and selectivity trade-offs.

Where it breaks

  • The code/weights situation is less mature than the rest of this catalogue; reproducibility is a risk.
  • Multi-target scores can be gamed by promiscuous or non-drug-like molecules.
  • Synthesizability, selectivity, and ADMET are not guaranteed by the generative objective.

How I would improve it

  • Release a pinned inference recipe with the exact checkpoint and target-encoding format.
  • Add synthesizability and selectivity constraints to the generation objective, not only docking scores.
  • Evaluate on prospective polypharmacology pairs with experimental data.
