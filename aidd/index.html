From structure to AIDD

I spent more than a decade determining protein structures and designing inhibitors. That work taught me what a model has to get right: chemistry, geometry, and the experiment that can falsify it. I now hold an M.S. in Computer Science (AI) from Georgia Tech, and I am building an AIDD practice around reproducible model evaluation, protein and antibody design, and wet-lab-aware filters.

This catalog is the evidence trail. The goal is not to run the most models. It is to know which ones are worth trusting, where they fail, and how to make them better.

First planned campaign

Open the RFantibody brief

Antibody design · 2,000 designs

RFantibody, then an honest filter.

The first campaign is a 2,000-design antibody run: antigen and epitope hotspots in, CDR loops and sequences out, then RF2 and Boltz-2 scoring before anything reaches the bench. The point is not the number of designs. The point is the funnel and the failures.

What I will record

  1. Exact commit, framework, CDR length ranges, and hotspot selection.
  2. Backbone, sequence, and complex-score distributions across all 2,000 designs.
  3. Top candidates, developability flags, and experimental results when available.

How to read a page

Every brief follows the same order: what it does, the AI principle, what it is good at, where it breaks, how I would improve it, and my test. The test section is deliberately empty until I run the model. When I add results, they will include commands, runtime, figures, and failures, not only the best numbers.
